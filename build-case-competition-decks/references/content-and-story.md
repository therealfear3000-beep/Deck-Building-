## 1. Source contract and ledger

Read the whole playbook before selecting its most memorable phrases. Distinguish source authority from presentation order:

| Input | Authority | Treatment |
|---|---|---|
| Competition brief/rubric | Required questions and constraints | Map every mandatory item to a visible slide |
| Approved playbook | Recommendations and intended argument | Preserve substance; edit expression and sequence |
| Case exhibits | Stated facts | Preserve period, unit, population, definitions |
| Supplied primary research | Actual observations | Preserve sample, method, and limitations |
| External research | Context and corroboration | Cite primary sources; check date/applicability |
| Reference decks | Communication examples | Transfer patterns, not claims, people, or assumptions |

Create a ledger, for example:

| ID | Claim/value | Base and period | Locator | Status | Use |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| C01 | 60 of 240 new hires left | First 12 months, cohort A | Playbook §2 table 1 | Case fact | Diagnosis |
| P01 | Offer previews at two sites | Proposed 90-day pilot | Playbook §4 | Proposal | Mechanism |
| A01 | Replacement cost ₹3 lakh | Per avoided exit | Playbook §6 | Assumption | Economics |
| M01 | 12 fewer exits × ₹3 lakh = ₹36 lakh | Illustrative annual cohort | Scenario and A01 | Model | Economics |

Do not present P01 as implemented or M01 as guaranteed savings. Explain the causal bridge from action to expected outcome; mark an untested bridge as a hypothesis. Keep missing data unknown or make it a visible sensitivity assumption.

If values conflict, identify the authoritative table or recalculate from inputs. If unresolved, omit the unsupported headline, show legitimate labelled scenarios, or ask one focused question if the decision cannot be represented honestly without it. Do not choose an optimistic value silently.

## 2. Narrative under a slide cap

Use a decision sequence:

**Answer → diagnosis → solution architecture → mechanisms → execution/ownership → economics → risks/gates → approval request.**

Introduce the recommendation early. Company facts earn space only if they change the decision. Introduce a framework only when it changes diagnosis, selection, or design. Use stable programme names.

| Core slide allowance | Useful structure, adapted to rubric |
|---|---|
| 3 | Diagnosis + answer; mechanism + differentiation; rollout + economics + gates |
| 5 | Answer/evidence; architecture; key mechanisms/segments; economics/risks; rollout/ask |
| 8 | Decision summary; diagnosis; architecture; two mechanism slides; business case; implementation; gates/ask |
| 10–12 | Add evidence, alternatives, personas, or governance where they resolve an objection |

Exclude cover from these counts only if competition rules permit. If all slides count, budget it or combine title and recommendation. Do not add a thank-you-only slide under a tight cap. Keep the decision, investment, and conditions visible during questions.

Do not hide mandatory material in a prohibited appendix or speaker notes. Under a hard cap, combine related functions and select the evidence required by the rubric. A diagram can integrate actors, handoffs, and checks. Preserve a visible qualification when it changes the decision.

## 3. Compression that preserves meaning

Use an information ladder:

1. **Takeaway title:** usually 8–16 words, one supported claim/action; at most two lines.
2. **Zone heading:** 2–6 words explaining that zone's job.
3. **Explanation:** 20–45 words or two/three concise bullets with complete meaning.
4. **Proof/decision:** number with base and period, mechanism, condition, or next action.
5. **Source:** short visible reference; full locator in notes/appendix.

Start with actor or action. Cut repetition, academic introductions, padding, and duplicate summaries. Expand acronyms on first use. Bold a few anchors rather than whole paragraphs. Retain “proposed”, “illustrative”, “may”, “surveyed”, and “versus baseline” when they change truth conditions.

### Keep the operating detail

Overwritten: “In order to address the issue of limited visibility regarding career opportunities, a systematic and structured discussion mechanism should be put in place on a regular quarterly basis involving line managers and human resources business partners.”

Too thin: “Career visibility. Manager enablement. Talent mobility.”

Slide-ready, if supported by the playbook:
> **Quarterly career reviews:** The line manager and HRBP agree one next-role goal, the evidence needed to qualify, and a dated development action. Review progress each quarter so employees can see how to advance.

Do not invent operating details while compressing. Label proposed additions to a playbook as additions.

### Preserve the limitation

Weak: “Employees reject rural jobs.”

Better: “In the 25-person student survey, only two respondents preferred fully rural roles. Test whether a realistic job preview improves acceptance among the target applicant cohort.”

This pairs evidence with action without generalizing a small exploratory sample.

### Keep an initiative complete across the deck

| Element | Question to answer |
|---|---|
| Problem | Which evidenced friction does this address? |
| Mechanism | What trigger, action, handoff, and result change? |
| Accountability | Who owns it, when, with what resources? |
| Proof | Which measure, comparison, and gate determine success? |

Do not repeat all four elements on every slide. Distribute them across architecture, mechanism, and implementation slides; use a completeness map so relocation does not delete them. Prefer a substantive 140-word slide to either a 400-word wall or four empty slogans.

## 4. Numerical controls

Use full precision for calculation and sensible rounding for display. Use one unit convention per table. State time horizons. Separate one-time cash, recurring spend, allocated staff time, revenue, margin contribution, realized savings, and non-monetized outcomes.

- `Net benefit = comparable-period incremental benefit − incremental cost`.
- `ROI (%) = net benefit / incremental cost × 100`.
- `Benefit–cost ratio = incremental benefit / incremental cost`.
- `Avoided exits = relevant headcount × absolute attrition reduction`.
- `Attrition-value estimate = avoided exits × incremental replacement cost per exit`.

Handle zero cost as undefined ROI. Distinguish a 3 percentage-point fall from a 3% relative fall. Do not count retention in both attrition and leadership savings unless distinct benefits are established. Do not count revenue as profit or productivity equivalents as cash without a realization mechanism.

Use conservative/base/upside scenarios where impact is uncertain. Show pilot validation and minimum acceptable impact. Never invent comparison scores or weights to make a recommendation win. Keep all repeated values consistent with one calculation source.

## 5. Storyboard specification

```text
Slide 4 — core / read-through
Judge question: How does this change the employee experience?
Title: One entry point routes employees to accountable support
Zones: Entry (20%); route/handoff (55%); controls/outcome (25%).
Visual: Native flow with a capacity gate and named handoff owners.
Visible copy: [write the final sentences, not placeholder instructions]
Claim IDs: C03, C08, P02; label target T01 visibly.
Notes: Full sources, assumptions, excluded alternatives, operating detail.
Objection: What happens if the receiving team has no capacity?
Resolution: Show the hold/re-route decision before handoff.
```

Check the title-only story and layout sequence before coding. Rewrite a slide that answers the wrong question instead of decorating it.
