## 1. Scope and limitations

Analyzed on 6 October 2026: all 39 pages of four supplied PDF decks, using extracted text, full-deck contact sheets, and full-size inspection of detailed slides. Page references below are PDF page numbers, not printed content-slide numbers. These are PDF exports: original PowerPoint object structure, grouping, animations, and editability cannot be verified. Font names suggest mixed authoring assets but do not prove an authoring application.

The user supplied these as MBA case competition references. Their competition results were not independently established. Do not label them winners merely because they are attractive. Two additional public decks have organizer-published winner provenance; their analysis below is selective, not a claim to audit their underlying business facts.

## 2. Cross-deck findings

All four use 16:9 canvases, recognizable branding, strong section headers, divided panels, emphasized words, and mixtures of diagrams, tables, icons, and short prose. Longer decks use main narratives followed by evidence appendices. The strongest content packages distinguish diagnosis, recommendation, mechanism, implementation, measurement, and economics.

However, the supplied slides are often dense despite being well divided:

|---|---:|---|---:|
| C_TISS_SEIRYU (1)(1).pdf | 6 | 2–5 | 445–647 |
| SwanSong_TISS_Conundrum(2).pdf | 15 | 2–10 | 262–512 |
| TISS HRM&LR_ TEAM POWERHOUSE.pdf | 4 | 2–4 | 344–375 |
| collabHRators_interrobang(2).pdf | 14 | 2–9 | 328–463 |

Counts are approximate text-extraction counts including labels, navigation, and source lines, not manually counted body copy; image text can be missed. They support a density critique, not a precise font prescription. All supplied PDFs have a 1440 × 810 point canvas; raw extracted font sizes should not be treated as standard widescreen PPT font sizes.

**Transfer:** information grouping, repeatable visual language, semantic diagrams, mechanism-specific details, explicit sources and assumptions.

**Improve:** overly small body text, large blocks of references, duplicate title/summary statements, too many saturated colours, logo clutter, complex pages with no dominant focal point, and numbers that do not reconcile.

## 3. Seiryu — a strong operating-model story, compressed too far

Identity: violet/white/black, prominent claim-led title band, pale violet panels, bottom progress chevrons, numbered page marker, repeated role icons. Diagrams encode system boundaries and decision rights rather than merely decorating the text.

| PDF page | Structure and content treatment | Retain | Improve |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Branded cover: photo at left, title/team at right, violet edge | Clear visual identity and restrained cover hierarchy | Keep sponsor template only when required; do not borrow unrelated branding |
| 2 | Diagnosis: metrics/comparison at left, employee breakdown at right, three gaps along bottom | Connect evidence to a named system failure; paired bars show differences | Too many diagnoses and restatements compete; isolate the decisive comparisons |
| 3 | Two process rails separated by privacy wall; actor column at right; promise strip below | Excellent use of geometry to explain boundaries, handoffs, and who acts | Many micro-panels; some handoff text visibly collides/overlaps; simplify or split |
| 4 | Personas left, shared entry/choice layer centre, human responsibilities right | Personas connect to different journeys while retaining common entitlement | Personal profiles, external statistics, routes, roles, and transitions crowd one slide |
| 5 | Four rollout phases with gates; scorecard, scenario model, dual approval keys, final ask | Explicit proposed targets, scenario assumptions, scale/stop logic, concrete request | Densest page; separate business case from roadmap if cap permits; distinguish India evidence from any local inference |
| 6 | Plain branded thank-you | Clean closing colour | Under tight caps, retain the decision request instead of consuming a slide |

Writing strengths: titles state an argument; language distinguishes team-level work conditions from personal care; proposals and assumptions are often explicitly labelled. Weaknesses: repeated prohibitions and emphatic contrast slogans consume space; dense explanatory qualifiers become unreadable. Preserve the boundary itself and its most consequential rule, then move exhaustive detail to notes/appendix. Do not re-use medical or privacy assertions as verified advice for a new case.

## 4. SwanSong — extensive evidence and execution detail, uneven hierarchy

Identity: purple/yellow/red brand cues, illustrated cover, lime active-section chevrons, rounded coloured section bars, dashed panel outlines, multi-colour tables, short bottom summaries. Body pages combine research evidence, mechanisms, and benchmarking; an appendix holds limitations and financial workings.

| PDF page | Structure and content treatment | Retain / improve |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Branded illustrated cover and portraits | Strong identity; keep portraits optional and authorized |
| 2 | Context column, six challenge cells, four solution quadrants | Useful diagnosis-to-response map; reduce background inventory |
| 3 | Seven-step research strip, interview quotes, small-sample survey results | Triangulated evidence and sample disclosure; shorten method strip and limit quotes |
| 4 | Three persona rows with motivators, inhibitors, EVP response | Make segmentation change design; simplify text in each cell |
| 5 | Benchmark column, recruitment flow, journal/platform and channel details | Specific operating ideas; divide multiple initiatives across slides |
| 6 | Large cross-company scholarship/fellowship comparison | Common comparison dimensions; promote only the deciding insight and move full table to appendix |
| 7 | Feeder institutes/partnerships left, teaching and staged innovation system right | Specific partners and progressive commitment; logos and multiple programmes overfill the page |
| 8 | Knowledge-sharing practice plus programme KPI tables | Action-to-measure linkage; consolidate repetitive benefits and label target status |
| 9 | Cost/benefit table, summary, prioritization chart, horizons, RACI | Economics plus ownership; too many visual tasks and an ROI terminology error |
| 10 | Multi-year Gantt above change actions and closing narrative | Sequence and change ownership; close with an explicit approval decision |
| 11 | Thank-you/Q&A screen | Use only if cap allows; decision slide is more useful during Q&A |
| 12 | Six limitations in a two-column grid | Admit adoption, measurement, and resource constraints; prioritize and add triggers/owners |
| 13 | Detailed assumption table | Keep formula inputs and rationale auditable; don't imply an assumption is externally proven |
| 14 | Cost workings beside benefit workings | Good audit trail; distinguish revenue, margin, savings, and time cost |
| 15 | Formula-and-value summary | Correctly separates net benefit, ROI, and benefit–cost ratio at initiative level |

Writing strengths: programmes have specific formats, participants, timing, and measures. Weaknesses: some titles name a topic rather than a conclusion; long academic references and self-referential summaries repeat content; aspirational outcomes sometimes sound certain. Small research samples should support hypotheses, not universal claims.

Numerical lesson: page 9 displays annual cost ₹647 lakh and benefit ₹973 lakh, then labels approximately 1.50×/150% as ROI. Those inputs imply **benefit–cost ratio ≈1.50× and net ROI ≈50.4%**, assuming costs and benefits are comparable. Page 15 correctly differentiates these concepts for a separate initiative. Reconcile headline and appendix calculations, time bases, and metric names; winning-looking design does not validate financial logic.

## 5. Powerhouse — disciplined three-slide logic, document-like type density

Identity: sponsor cover; top three-stage navigation; navy, teal, gold and red headers; pastel tables; serif analytical body; a few small cue icons; persistent sponsor footer. Three content slides tell a coherent diagnosis → architecture → operating-model/value story.

| PDF page | Structure and content treatment | Retain / improve |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Full sponsor cover | Respect mandatory cover; do not imitate sponsor endorsement elsewhere |
| 2 | Company snapshot + answer, portfolio comparison, evidence cards and implication | Business differences drive design; reduce table copy and make the decision-changing dimension prominent |
| 3 | Eight capabilities grouped into three mandates; each has action, decision, metric | Parallel wording makes a large set comparable; preserve eight when the brief requires eight; enlarge type and simplify hierarchy |
| 4 | Decision-rights table, three business pilots, benefits chain, KPI dashboard | Strong owners and differentiated pilots; multiple governance tables need compression or an allowed appendix |

Writing strengths: precise capability definitions, explicit decisions and metrics, and visible “hypothesis to validate” markers. Weaknesses: generic section headings carry more visual weight than the answer; numerous small rows demand close reading. Several scale/stop conditions require operational thresholds before execution. Retain business-specific logic, not the exact density or small serif text.

## 6. collabHRators — clear programme architecture, important consistency issues

Identity: textured branded cover, navy section bars, lime active navigation, pastel grids, mixed colourful icons, consistent bottom source/summary bands. Main story moves from attrition diagnosis and theory to hiring, growth, enablement, rollout, and financial limitations; appendices show research and tools.

| PDF page | Structure and content treatment | Retain / improve |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Team/competition cover | Clear identity; neutralize texture if it competes with text |
| 2 | Context left, challenge grid, driver diagram and four-part answer right | Connect business issue to a named solution; reduce eight simultaneous mini-problems |
| 3 | Generational/external evidence, pipeline diagnosis, survey/quotes | Mixed evidence sources; mark small samples and avoid causal overreach |
| 4 | Job embeddedness, EVP/theory matrix, four-part solution | Frameworks translate into moves; shorten theory to its design implication |
| 5 | Survey insight, sourcing rules, realistic previews, selection tools, senior EVP | Specific mechanisms and target groups; too many programmes and a large QR reduce focus |
| 6 | Role expansion left; readiness gates, fairness rules, rotation below/right | Strong visual separation of growth, selection and controls; reduce icon styles and table density |
| 7 | IDP comparison and recognition by level | Clear segmentation with actors/cadence; replace generic benefits with specific outcomes |
| 8 | Data → model → signal → response above a Gantt | Connect signal to an accountable action; risk scores need validation and lawful/fair inputs, not visual authority |
| 9 | Costs/benefits left, limitations and conclusion right | Pair value with limitations; reconcile benefit figures before making the ROI claim |
| 10 | Branded thank-you | Keep only if useful within cap |
| 11 | Eight survey questions/charts | Useful backup evidence; native bars and visible n improve readability over chart mosaics |
| 12 | Industry survey charts | Preserve base and method; do not generalize a 13-person sample |
| 13 | Financial assumptions/workings | Make calculations auditable; fix inconsistent line items and arithmetic |
| 14 | Six-part interview tool with questions/probes/criteria | Operational appendix is valuable; review role relevance and sensitive-question appropriateness before applying |

Writing strengths: specific triggers, actions, actors, and mechanisms; programme names are stable; main/appendix separation is useful. Weaknesses: repeated framework language, unexplained acronyms, overstated causal promises, and descriptions of work done instead of takeaways.

Numerical lesson: page 9 shows attrition benefit ₹360 lakh, whereas page 13 shows ₹180 lakh. Other benefits are ₹250/100/300 lakh. The displayed ₹260 lakh net benefit and ~45.6% ROI align with the appendix's ₹830 lakh total benefits and ₹570 lakh costs, not the main page's benefit line items. The IDP rationale also does not cleanly reconcile ₹1 lakh × 50 people with a ₹100 lakh line without an explicit remaining cost. Flag and resolve such discrepancies instead of copying polished numbers.

## 7. Verified public winning references

### Rotman “The Big Four” — Smith National MBA Case Competition 2025

Winner provenance: [official Smith results](https://smith.queensu.ca/mba_programs/mba/case-competition/index.php) names this team first place and links [The Arctic Bridge PDF](https://smith.queensu.ca/mba_programs/mba/case-competition/the-arctic-bridge.pdf). Inspected its 13-page contact sheet and representative slides. Accessed 6 October 2026.

Observed: restrained pale-blue background; large spaced headings; compact progress navigation; a three-pillar summary (p4); a diagram emphasizing shared infrastructure (p6); a funding chart/table pair (p11); a four-phase timeline (p12). Layout variety supports the argument. Improve tiny navigation, topic-style titles, text-heavy sections, and traceability of quantified assertions. Transfer the deliberate dominant visual and separation of summary, mechanism, financing, and timing; do not import its historical assumptions. Winner status does not prove which design feature caused the outcome.

### HKUST — Marshall International Case Competition 2019

Winner provenance: [official Marshall archive](https://marshallinternationalcasecomp.squarespace.com/past-competitions) names HKUST as 2019 winner and links [the Zuru print deck](https://static1.squarespace.com/static/57630ec72e69cf954a891ca7/t/5cb79ce9eef1a1ddad83df7d/1555537132134/HKUST%2BMICC%2B2019%2BPrint%2BDeck.pdf). This is a broader international case-competition reference, not established here as an MBA deck. Accessed 6 October 2026.

Observed: 27 PDF pages contain 53 slide frames, including progressive builds; do not mistake this for 53 unique arguments. Inspected representative diagnosis, mechanism, implementation, financial, and appendix pages. Blue/white styling, assertion headlines, orange emphasis, charts, criterion comparisons, customer journeys, and roadmap/metric pages provide useful layout variety. Slides 41–46 connect implementation, targets, financial drivers, and risks. Transfer that reasoning sequence and appendix traceability; avoid copying repeated build frames into a static submission or presenting subjective scores as objective facts.

## 8. Rules derived from the analysis

| Observed strength or failure | Skill rule |
|---|---|
| Readable grouping despite high total word count | Keep labelled divisions but enforce a lower density and larger body type |
| Seiryu's boundaries and Powerhouse's business differences | Let the diagram encode the distinctive mechanism |
| collabHRators' gates and SwanSong's programme details | Preserve actions, owners, timing, and tests during compression |
| Useful appendices | Keep headline slides readable while preserving evidence and calculations when appendices are allowed |
| Financial inconsistencies | Use one source ledger and recalculate all repeated metrics |
| Many colours, icons, outlines and footers | Reduce visual grammar to one consistent system |
| Public winners use different styles | Adapt to the case; there is no universal winning template |
| PDF exports obscure original objects | Build and verify native PPT editability explicitly |
