# Visual system and readable density

Use the supplied brand template when required. Otherwise create a restrained system informed by the reference decks, with less visual noise and larger body type. The measurements below are design heuristics, not empirically established winning thresholds.

## Canvas and hierarchy

| Element | Balanced read-through default on 13.333 × 7.5 inches |
|---|---|
| Outer margin | 0.40–0.50 in |
| Section indicator | 0.20–0.30 in high; short label or 4–6 compact sections |
| Title | 28–34 pt; 8–16 words, at most two lines |
| Optional subtitle | 16–18 pt, only if it adds a condition or context |
| Panel headings | 18–21 pt, consistent sentence case |
| Body | 16–19 pt; prefer 17–18, never shrink simply to meet a cap |
| Diagram/chart/table labels | 14–17 pt; prefer 16 for key content |
| Source/footer | 9–11 pt; no decision-critical facts at this size |
| Panel gutter | 0.20–0.30 in; internal padding 0.15–0.22 in |
| Content area | Roughly y=1.40 to 6.55; adapt for one/two-line title |
| Takeaway strip | Optional 0.38–0.50 in; use only if it adds implication |

For live delivery, use 20–24 pt body and fewer elements. Scale the grid, not just the font, for another aspect ratio. Do not copy font sizes from PDF metadata: the supplied PDFs use a 1440 × 810 canvas, so PDF point sizes do not translate directly into standard widescreen PPT point sizes.

Use left-aligned explanatory text. Reserve centred text for short labels, titles inside nodes, and large metrics. Limit line length by splitting into zones; do not create long, shallow full-width paragraphs. Avoid all-caps body text and vertically rotated explanations. Ensure visibly sufficient contrast; use a contrast checker when colours are uncertain. Do not use colour alone for readiness or risk—include labels, symbols, or both.

## Layout rhythm

Use 2–4 major zones, not a tile for every sentence. Prefer a 60/40 or 65/35 split for evidence plus implication, three equal columns for genuinely parallel levers, stacked bands for phases, and a dominant central schematic for an operating model. Treat nested subparts as subordinate, with lighter borders and smaller headings.

Make reading order obvious: headline → evidence/mechanism → implication/decision. At thumbnail scale the reader should see the dominant idea, not dozens of similarly weighted rectangles. At normal reading size the reader should understand the mechanism without zooming into microtext.

Keep whitespace between groups. Do not decorate every edge with dashed boxes, heavy outlines, logos, tabs, and coloured bars. Use either light fills or borders for most panels. Place navigation consistently and highlight only the active section. For three-slide submissions, a three-stage ribbon can efficiently signal the whole story; for longer decks, a small chapter label can be cleaner.

## Colour tokens

Pick a palette relevant to the case rather than inheriting every reference colour. Use a dark ink, neutral background, muted panel fill, one primary, and one supporting accent. Keep status colours separate from brand colours.

| Theme example | Ink | Primary | Accent | Surface | Use |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Violet/teal | 20243A | 542785 | 007C83 | F3F1F7 | People strategy, operating model |
| Navy/amber | 142B49 | 1D4F91 | B96A09 | F2F5F8 | Portfolio, commercial case |
| Charcoal/blue | 24262B | 176D9C | 147D75 | F0F5F9 | Infrastructure, technology |

Choose white or dark foreground after checking contrast. Do not set tiny white text on pale bright fills. Reuse a solution's assigned colour across architecture, roadmap, and metrics. Never imply ordinal scoring with arbitrary colours without a legend.

## Icons, illustrations, and stickers

Use visuals with a specific role:

- **Identity:** one small, supplied company/competition logo in a stable position.
- **Navigation:** one consistent icon per pillar, reused throughout.
- **Explanation:** gate for a decision threshold, person for an actor, lock for a boundary, arrows for actual movement.
- **Human context:** a small persona illustration beside a stated need and journey.
- **Evidence:** genuine product/site image, map, or mockup when visual context matters.

Use one icon family with consistent stroke, scale, and colours. Prefer native shapes for simple icons. Keep complex stickers as images only if needed; keep captions and business labels separate and editable. Disclose the image exception if promising editability. Use zero to two decorative illustrations on most analytical slides; more repeated actor icons are acceptable in a meaningful diagram.

Avoid mixing emoji, clipart, detailed stock photos, and outline icons on one page. Avoid celebration imagery in analytical panels. Do not use a logo as proof of a partnership or endorsement. Do not copy reference-team headshots or names. Preserve a mandated competition cover if supplied; otherwise use a concise title, recommendation, team identity, and one relevant visual.

## Compression order when crowded

1. Remove duplicate summary and repeated background.
2. Convert prose relationships into a labelled diagram or comparison.
3. Reduce the number of simultaneous messages and evidence items.
4. Move supporting tables/calculations to an allowed appendix.
5. Split a slide if the cap allows.
6. Rebalance zones and shorten copy while preserving meaning.
7. Adjust type only within the intended readable range, then re-render.

Never solve crowding by flattening text into an image, hiding essential qualifications, or shrinking body text into footnotes.
