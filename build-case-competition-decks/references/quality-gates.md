# Final quality gates

Use these gates to decide whether the deck is ready. This is a quality rubric, not a predictor or guarantee of a competition win.

## Gate 1 — Faithful and defensible content

- All required brief/rubric questions are answered visibly within the permitted cap.
- The recommendation and named programmes match the approved playbook.
- Every quantitative or material external claim has a traceable source.
- Facts, inferences, assumptions, targets, and scenarios remain distinct on the relevant slides.
- No invented interviews, survey findings, quotes, benchmark numbers, or guaranteed outcomes appear.
- Small-sample findings retain n and scope. Illustrative personas are labelled.
- Headline, body, chart, notes, and appendix values reconcile.
- Arithmetic, units, periods, bases, percentage points, ROI, and benefit–cost ratio are correct.

Do not pass this gate with an unresolved number central to the decision. Remove the unsupported claim, represent valid alternatives honestly, or obtain the missing clarification.

## Gate 2 — Complete decision logic

- Reading only titles conveys the answer, rationale, mechanism, execution, and decision.
- The diagnosis distinguishes symptoms from plausible causes.
- Each recommendation changes a specific actor's action, process, or resource allocation.
- Ownership, timing, resources, and success/stop criteria exist across the deck.
- Relevant alternatives and trade-offs are acknowledged.
- Financial value does not substitute for capacity, adoption, or other essential controls.
- The last substantive slide asks for a concrete next decision.

## Gate 3 — Clear and substantial writing

- Each slide has one principal takeaway and 2–4 distinct zones when warranted.
- Each zone says enough to be understood without reading the playbook.
- Text uses concrete actors and verbs, not slogans or unexplained acronyms.
- Essential qualifiers survive compression.
- Repeated company context, duplicate conclusions, and academic padding are removed.
- The word-density range is used as a review signal, not a quota or rigid rule.

Read each slide aloud as a short explanation. If it requires inventing missing links, improve the copy. If it repeats itself, cut it. If it is an empty set of labels, restore the mechanism.

## Gate 4 — Visual clarity

- The contact sheet shows consistent identity and appropriate layout variety.
- Each slide has a clear reading order and dominant visual/message.
- All required text is legible at the intended viewing size.
- No clipping, unintended overlap, orphaned labels, broken wrapping, or missing glyphs remain.
- Tables are comparable and readable; charts have units, periods, bases, and actual/target labels.
- Diagrams express the real relationship and do not invent causal certainty.
- Arrows, status colours, and size encodings have consistent meaning.
- Icons aid explanation; photos/stickers do not crowd the argument.
- Footer references are short; long locators remain accessible in notes/appendix.

## Gate 5 — Editable and usable delivery

- A real PPTX exists and reopens successfully.
- Business text, diagrams, tables and charts use native editable objects.
- Quantitative chart data is embedded and can be updated.
- Any image-only photos/logos/illustrations are the intended exceptions.
- Structural errors are fixed and warnings are reviewed.
- Every slide was rendered and inspected, or rendering limitations are explicitly disclosed.
- A temporary editability copy was checked for the relevant object types.
- Final files are saved through the environment's durable artifact workflow and linked clearly.

## Review scoring when useful

Assign 0 (missing/incorrect), 1 (usable with material weakness), or 2 (strong) to fidelity, decision logic, writing, visual clarity, and editability. A score can help prioritize revisions but must not override a failed hard gate. Do not polish decorative details while a number, decision, or editable object remains broken.
